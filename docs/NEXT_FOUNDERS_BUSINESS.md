# Founder hypothesis — not customer evidence

**Initial buyer:** independent training providers repeatedly delivering text-heavy PowerPoint lessons in classrooms/training halls.

**Job to be done:** assess supported text-height geometry for the room, review bounded changes, and download an independently checked copy before a session. The product does not guarantee visual readability or universal Office compatibility.

**Why this buyer rather than conference organizers?** Recurring text-heavy training work matches current single-deck, limited-size, single-replica workflow. Large conferences need batch intake, security review and support for graphics-heavy decks that do not exist yet.

**Revenue options to investigate:** limited free evaluation, paid credits per supported deck, monthly trainer credits. Exact prices, customer lifetime value and conversion rate remain hypotheses.

**Costs to measure:** per-deck render CPU and wall time, paid Gemini invocation (if configured), failed-job cost, hosting and storage, customer support, processing fees, and commercial licensing implications for PyMuPDF.

**Customer discovery template:** Interview three to five actual training providers. Ask when slides have been too small, how they currently fix them, number of events and decks per month, willingness to share non-confidential files, reaction to review-only cases and what would justify paying. Record objections faithfully. Don't turn interviews into invented testimonials.

**Financial model:** contribution/job = collected price/job − compute − model − storage/bandwidth − payment fees − attributable support. Treat projected users and income as scenarios, not traction. Compliance/legal costs and fixed overhead remain additional.

**Key falsifier:** low useful-job rate on independently selected supported training decks, slower-than-manual correction with no meaningful accuracy benefit, or unwillingness to upload real material. Do not spend on payment infrastructure before addressing it.
